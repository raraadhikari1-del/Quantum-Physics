01 — What is Quantum Mechanics?

Quantum mechanics is a theory used to describe the behavior of matter and energy at very small scales. It becomes especially important when studying atoms, electrons, photons, and other microscopic particles.

Classical physics works extremely well for many everyday situations, but it cannot completely explain certain microscopic phenomena. Quantum mechanics was developed to explain these observations and introduced a new way of thinking about nature.

02 — Quantization of Energy

One of the important ideas in quantum mechanics is that certain physical quantities can only have specific allowed values. Energy, for example, can exist in discrete levels rather than taking every possible value.

This idea was important in explaining the behavior of atoms. Electrons in an atom can occupy particular energy levels, and they can move between these levels by absorbing or releasing specific amounts of energy.

03 — Wave-Particle Duality

Quantum objects can display properties associated with both particles and waves. Light, for example, can behave like a wave in experiments involving interference, but it can also interact with matter as individual packets of energy called photons.

Matter can also show wave-like behavior. Electrons and other particles can produce interference and diffraction patterns, demonstrating that the microscopic world cannot always be described using the classical idea of a tiny solid particle.

04 — Superposition

Superposition is the principle that a quantum system can be described as a combination of multiple possible states until a measurement is made. These possibilities are represented mathematically by a quantum state.

Superposition is one of the reasons quantum systems behave so differently from ordinary objects. It is also an important idea behind technologies such as quantum computing.

05 — Heisenberg Uncertainty Principle

The uncertainty principle describes a fundamental limit on how precisely certain pairs of physical quantities can be known at the same time. Position and momentum are a famous example.

The principle does not simply mean that our instruments are imperfect. Instead, it is a fundamental feature of quantum systems.

06 — Quantum Entanglement

Quantum entanglement occurs when two or more quantum systems become described by a shared quantum state. Measurements on one part of an entangled system are correlated with measurements on the other part.

Entanglement has become an important concept in modern physics and plays a major role in areas such as quantum communication and quantum information science.

07 — Quantum Tunneling

Quantum tunneling is a phenomenon in which a quantum particle can have a probability of appearing on the other side of a potential barrier, even when classical physics would predict that the particle cannot cross it.

Tunneling is not just a theoretical idea. It plays an important role in several physical processes and technologies, including certain types of electronic devices and scanning tunneling microscopes.

08 — The Schrödinger Equation

The Schrödinger equation is one of the central equations of quantum mechanics. It describes how the quantum state of a system changes and allows physicists to calculate possible outcomes and their probabilities.

A central object in this description is the wavefunction, usually represented by the symbol Ψ. The wavefunction contains information about the quantum state of a system.

09 — Quantum Computing

Quantum computing is a next-generation technology that uses the strange rules of quantum physics to solve complex problems faster than traditional computers .By using qubits instead of standard bits, these systems can process massive amounts of data simultaneously. This allows them to tackle challenges in fields like cryptography, medicine, and artificial intelligence that are currently impossible for regular supercomputers to solve.
